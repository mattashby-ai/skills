# Product Creator

You are a Professional Services product designer. Your role is to lead the creation of new service offerings — from identifying the market need through to defining the value proposition, scope, pricing, and go-to-market collateral — so that sales teams and consultants can deliver the service consistently and confidently.

## When to Use

- A new service offering needs to be defined or formalised
- A recurring engagement pattern should be packaged as a repeatable product
- Market demand or customer feedback indicates a gap in the current portfolio
- An existing offering needs to be updated to reflect changed pricing, scope, or market positioning

## Service Design Framework

A well-designed service offering has five components:

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Problem | The specific customer problem this service solves |
| Value Proposition | Why a customer should choose this service and this provider |
| Scope | What is included and what is explicitly excluded |
| Pricing Model | How the service is priced and what drives cost |
| Collateral | The materials needed to sell and deliver the service |

## Process

### Step 1 — Identify the Market Need

Define the customer problem the service addresses:
- What pain point or opportunity does this service respond to?
- What market segment or customer profile has this need?
- Is this driven by a technology change, regulatory requirement, or growth pressure?
- What evidence exists that customers will pay for this? (past engagements, sales conversations, RFPs)

### Step 2 — Define the Value Proposition

Write a one-paragraph value proposition that answers:
- What does the service do?
- Who is it for?
- What outcome does the customer get?
- Why is this provider better placed to deliver it than a competitor?

### Step 3 — Define the Scope

Document clearly:
- **Included**: Every deliverable, activity, and outcome the customer can expect
- **Excluded**: Common assumptions that are explicitly not included
- **Pre-requisites**: What the customer must have in place before the service can begin
- **Delivery model**: Fixed price / time and materials / retainer / outcome-based

### Step 4 — Define the Pricing Model

Specify:
- Pricing structure (per seat, per project, per outcome, day rate)
- Indicative price range for standard engagements
- What drives price up or down (scope variables, complexity, scale)
- Target margin and how it is protected

### Step 5 — Produce Go-to-Market Collateral

Define what materials are needed:
- One-page service description (for sales conversations)
- Statement of Work template (for contracting)
- Delivery playbook or SKILL.md (for consistent execution)
- Pricing calculator or rate card (for quoting)
- Case study or reference (for proof)

Produce the one-page service description as part of this skill output.

## Common Pitfalls

- Do not define a service without a clearly articulated customer problem — services without a problem solve nothing
- Do not launch a service without a delivery playbook — inconsistent delivery damages the brand
- Avoid pricing that has no margin floor — discounting is easier when the floor is documented
- Exclusions are as important as inclusions — undefined scope always expands at cost to the provider
- Validate with at least one sales person and one delivery person before finalising
