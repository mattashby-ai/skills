## Using the Rate Card for Opportunity Qualification

During qualification, the rate card is used to assess whether the customer's budget is sufficient to fund a commercially viable engagement.

### Budget Sufficiency Check

1. Estimate the engagement effort in days (rough order of magnitude)
2. Apply the likely role mix (e.g. 80% Consultant, 20% Senior Consultant)
3. Calculate the T&M cost at standard rates
4. Check whether the customer's stated or implied budget exceeds this estimate at 40% margin

**Blended rate examples:**

| Team Composition | Blended Day Rate |
|-----------------|----------------|
| All Engineers | $1,640/day |
| 50% Senior Engineers, 50% Consultant | $1,940/day |
| PA + 2× Senior Engineers | $1,827/day (blended) |
| Mixed delivery team (typical) | $1,500–$2,000/day |

### Minimum Viable Engagement

- Minimum engagement: 5 days ($7,000–$9,000 at standard rates)
- Engagements below $5,000 are generally not commercially viable after overhead
- Fixed-price engagements: add 15–20% contingency to T&M estimate

### Budget Red Flags

- Customer states a budget that is less than 60% of the T&M estimate → walk-away or descope conversation
- Customer refuses to confirm any budget → Medium qualification score at best (score 1 on Budget dimension)
- Customer budget was set 12+ months ago with no indexing → validate it still covers current scope

## Commercial Guardrails

- **Minimum margin:** 40% — do not qualify an opportunity as viable if it cannot be delivered at this margin
- **Discounting:** Any discount below standard rates requires Practice Lead approval
- **Partner solutions:** Resale of partner products (Microsoft, AWS, AvePoint, Wiz, Zscaler) is priced separately from PS services; partner margin is additional to PS margin
