# Professional Services Pricing Models — Reference

Use this file during Phase 3 of a commercial review. Identify the pricing model in use, assess fit, and flag issues if the model is clearly problematic for the engagement type.

---

## The Five Common Models

### 1. Hourly / Time & Materials (T&M)
**How it works**: Client pays for actual hours logged at an agreed rate. Costs are variable.

**Best fit for**: Small tasks, exploratory work, advisory engagements, or projects where scope cannot be defined upfront.

**Commercial risks to check**:
- Are billable and non-billable activities defined? (PM time, meetings, reviews)
- Is there a rate card or blended rate, and is it locked for the contract term?
- Is there a not-to-exceed (NTE) cap? Without one, client bears unlimited cost risk.
- Is time reporting required (frequency, format, approval process)?

**Red flags**: No NTE cap on long engagements; no definition of what counts as billable; no approval required before hours are logged against new work.

---

### 2. Fixed Fee
**How it works**: A single agreed price for a defined scope. Vendor bears cost overrun risk.

**Best fit for**: Standardised or repeatable services with well-understood scope (e.g. predefined implementation packages, defined audits).

**Commercial risks to check**:
- Is scope tightly defined with explicit exclusions? Vague scope on a fixed fee shifts risk to the vendor.
- Is there a change control / variation clause? Without one, scope creep is unrecoverable.
- Are acceptance criteria defined? Ambiguous acceptance = potential for endless revision.
- Does the fixed price include expenses, or are they billed separately?

**Red flags**: Fixed fee on exploratory or highly collaborative work; no change control mechanism; acceptance criteria undefined or subjective.

---

### 3. Retainer
**How it works**: Client pays a recurring fee (monthly/quarterly) for ongoing access to the vendor's team or capacity, regardless of output volume.

**Best fit for**: Continuous or recurring services — ongoing support, advisory, managed services — where demand is steady but not project-defined.

**Commercial risks to check**:
- Is the retainer scoped to a capacity allocation (e.g. X hours/month) or to defined outputs?
- What happens to unused capacity — does it roll over, lapse, or accumulate?
- Are out-of-scope requests handled via change order or absorbed?
- Are termination and notice terms reasonable? (Retainers can lock clients in.)

**Red flags**: No scope or capacity definition in a retainer; no mechanism for handling out-of-scope requests; auto-renewal clauses with short cancellation windows.

---

### 4. Project-Based / Milestone
**How it works**: Payment is tied to completion of defined project milestones rather than time elapsed or hours worked. A form of fixed fee structured around deliverable gates.

**Best fit for**: Multi-phase implementations, software delivery, construction-adjacent projects — where phased delivery and clear handoffs make sense.

**Commercial risks to check**:
- Are milestone definitions objective and verifiable? ("Phase 1 complete" is not sufficient.)
- Do milestones correspond to genuine value transfer, not administrative checkpoints?
- What is the process if a milestone is disputed? Is payment withheld or escrowed pending resolution?
- Is the final payment milestone weighted appropriately? (Large back-loaded payments incentivise vendor to rush; excessive upfront payments increase client risk.)

**Red flags**: Milestone definitions that are subjective or entirely vendor-controlled; back-loaded payment structure with no holdback mechanism; no dispute process for milestone acceptance.

---

### 5. Value-Based Pricing
**How it works**: Pricing is set relative to the value delivered to the client (e.g. % of cost savings, % of revenue uplift, outcome-linked fees) rather than effort or deliverables.

**Best fit for**: High-certainty outcome engagements — performance improvement programs, cost reduction initiatives — where the outcome is measurable and attributable.

**Commercial risks to check**:
- How is value defined and measured? Is the measurement methodology agreed in advance?
- Who controls the variables that determine value? (If the client controls outcomes, the vendor is exposed.)
- Is there a base fee, or is all compensation contingent? Pure contingency models are high risk for vendors.
- What is the audit/verification process for value calculation?

**Red flags**: No agreed measurement methodology; value metrics controlled entirely by one party; no floor or base fee; no cap on upside.

---

## Assessing Model-Scope Fit

| Engagement Type | Appropriate Models | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| Exploratory / undefined scope | T&M with NTE cap | Avoid fixed fee |
| Defined, repeatable deliverable | Fixed fee | Ensure change control exists |
| Ongoing support / advisory | Retainer with capacity definition | Define scope boundary |
| Multi-phase implementation | Milestone-based | Ensure objective acceptance criteria |
| Outcome-linked program | Value-based | Only if outcome is measurable and attributable |
| Hybrid (defined + ongoing) | Fixed fee + retainer | Clearly separate the two scopes |

---

## Output Guidance

If raising a pricing model issue, use this format in the review:

```
[RISK: PRICING MODEL]
Model in use: [identified model]
Issue: [specific problem — mismatch, missing control, or structural risk]
Affected clauses: [reference if applicable]
Recommendation: [alternative model or specific clause to add/amend]
```

Only raise a pricing model finding if the issue is material. Do not flag minor or stylistic differences from standard practice.
